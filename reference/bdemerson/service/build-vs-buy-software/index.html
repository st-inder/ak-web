Build vs Buy Software

Most software you should buy. A narrower set now pays to build, and AI moved that line. Model your initiative with the calculator, then pressure-test the result with a team that delivers both paths.
Open the calculator
Definition

What is a build vs buy analysis?

A build vs buy analysis compares the total cost of owning custom software against licensing a commercial product, then weighs what the spreadsheet misses: strategic role, product fit, compliance, team capability, and time to market. The math changed because AI-assisted delivery cut the cost of designing, building, and testing custom systems while license prices keep compounding the other way. The answer did not change for everything. Commodity tools and market-leader platforms are still a buy. The shift concentrates in vertical point solutions, the systems with six-figure licenses, thin vendor markets, and weak fit.

The recommendation weighs cost and context together. It is directional, not a formal business case.

How the model works. The cost model mirrors a standard TCO comparison. For buy: initial implementation, an annual license compounding at the SaaS increase rate plus a mid-life upgrade and license jump, and internal maintenance growing with labor inflation. For build: upfront design and build plus an optional one-off AI build and compute cost, maintenance growing with labor inflation, infrastructure and AI-token costs growing at the SaaS rate, and a mid-life upgrade. On top of the numbers, the model applies context: system type, strategic role, off-the-shelf fit, feature-parity ambition, effective license cost per user, compliance, in-house capability, and time to market. Commodity software and market-leader rebuilds are ruled out on principle, whatever the raw numbers say. Every driver behind a recommendation is shown so you can check the reasoning.

Build vs buy assessment

A structured pass over one initiative: a ten-year TCO model, an off-the-shelf fit review, and a written recommendation with the drivers stated. Fixed scope, typically two to three weeks.

TCO and license modeling

License curves with real renewal behavior: annual increases, upgrade-year jumps, and seat growth on one side; build, maintenance, infrastructure, and AI token costs on the other, over the same horizon.

Fit and requirements analysis

Requirements ranked by what differentiates you, then scored against the product short list. The output names the 20% worth owning and the 80% worth buying.

AI-assisted custom builds

Design, build, and test with AI agents doing the repetitive engineering under senior review. Smaller teams, shorter timelines, and testing depth that used to be unaffordable.

Our approach

How the assessment works

01

Frame the initiative

System type, strategic role, seats, compliance constraints, and time to market get pinned down first, because they decide more than the spreadsheet does.

02

Model the ten-year cost

The license curve with real renewal behavior on one side; build, maintenance, infrastructure, and AI costs on the other. Ranges where inputs are uncertain, with the driver named.

03

Score the fit

Requirements ranked by differentiation, then scored against the product short list. The gap between what you need and what ships out of the box is the heart of the decision.

04

Price the risks

Delivery risk, migration effort, edge-case testing, and the run cost after go-live. This is where weak build cases die honestly and strong ones survive scrutiny.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

When does building custom software make sense?

When the system is a vertical point solution or a true differentiator: the license runs six figures a year, off-the-shelf fit is below about 70%, and the capability sits close to how you win. AI-assisted delivery lowered build and maintain costs enough that this category flipped. Commodity tools did not flip, and market-leader platforms did not either.

Is it cheaper to build or buy software?

Buying is cheaper for commodity software at vendor-scale prices, at any seat count. Building becomes cheaper over a ten-year horizon when licenses are large and compounding, fit is weak, and the build is scoped to what you need rather than a vendor's full feature set. When building wins at all, the crossover usually lands between year three and year six.

What software should you never build?

Anything a vendor amortizes across millions of seats: email, documents, chat, office suites, commodity HR and accounting tools. A $20-per-user product would cost far more than $20 per user to build and maintain, and that stays true at 10,000 seats. Rebuilding market leaders like Salesforce or SAP fails the same test.

How does AI change the build vs buy decision?

AI-assisted design, coding, and testing cut the labor cost and timeline that made custom builds risky, and they make deep testing affordable, which is where custom projects historically failed. AI does not remove the need for rigorous requirements, clean data migration, or a support model. Model the effect directly by lowering the design-and-build input in the calculator.

What is a build vs buy analysis?

A structured comparison of owning custom software against licensing a product: ten-year total cost of ownership on both sides, plus strategic role, product fit, compliance, capability, and time to market. The output is a recommendation with the drivers stated, so the decision survives scrutiny from finance and the board.

How accurate is the calculator on this page?

It is directional by design. The cost model is a standard TCO comparison and the context weights are shown in the results, but it runs on your estimates, not validated vendor quotes or a detailed fit study. Treat the verdict as a filter for whether a full business case is worth commissioning.

What does a custom system cost to maintain?

Plan on 15 to 25% of the initial build cost per year across maintenance, enhancements, infrastructure, and AI tokens, varying with system criticality and rate of change. The calculator carries these as explicit inputs, because underpricing the run cost is the most common way build decisions go wrong.

Will you just tell us to build?

No. We implement commercial platforms and build custom software, and we get paid either way, so the assessment has no reason to lean. When buy wins, the recommendation names the product, and we can implement it.

Which systems make the strongest build cases?

Vertical point solutions: policy administration and claims in insurance, loan origination and servicing in financial services, MES and plant systems in manufacturing, and similar systems with few vendors, six-figure licenses, and weak fit to your process. These markets never had the scale economics that make commodity software unbeatable.

How long does an AI-assisted custom build take?

Small systems ship in 8 to 16 weeks. A point-solution replacement typically runs 4 to 9 months, driven by integrations and migration complexity more than by code volume. The assessment produces a range with the drivers named, not a single optimistic date.

What happens after the build ships?

The system moves to a run agreement: defined SLAs, security patching, an enhancement backlog, and cost monitoring. Custom software without a support model becomes legacy fast, so we price the run cost into the original business case.
