HIPAA Compliance Checklist: Privacy, Security, and Breach Rules

Compliance
/
October 4, 2026

A HIPAA compliance checklist covers five areas: the Privacy Rule, the Security Rule, the Breach Notification Rule, business associate agreements, and the training, policies, and documentation that prove the rest, with a current risk analysis at the center of the Security Rule. HIPAA applies to covered entities, meaning health plans, health care clearinghouses, and health care providers that transmit health information electronically in standard transactions such as claims, and to the business associates that handle protected health information (PHI) for them. No government agency certifies HIPAA compliance, so the checklist doubles as your evidence file: each line below names the requirement, its citation in 45 CFR Part 164, and the record that shows you meet it.

The tables reflect the regulations as of October 2026.

Who does HIPAA apply to?

HIPAA binds two groups, both defined in 45 CFR 160.103; confirm which one you are first.

GroupDefinition in 45 CFR 160.103Examples
Covered entity"A health plan," "a health care clearinghouse," or "a health care provider who transmits any health information in electronic form in connection with a transaction covered by this subchapter"Hospitals, practices, pharmacies, and labs that bill electronically; health insurers and employer group health plans; billing clearinghouses
Business associateA person who, on behalf of a covered entity, "creates, receives, maintains, or transmits protected health information" for a regulated function or activitySaaS and cloud vendors that store PHI, billing companies, managed IT providers, and law or accounting firms with PHI access
SubcontractorA subcontractor that creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of a business associate is itself a business associateA SaaS vendor's cloud host, support outsourcer, or backup provider
Outside HIPAAOrganizations that hold health information without one of these rolesA fitness app sold directly to consumers; an employer holding sick leave records as employer

Encryption does not take a cloud vendor out of scope. HHS's cloud computing guidance says "Lacking an encryption key does not exempt a CSP from business associate status."

The HIPAA compliance checklist at a glance

The table summarizes each area, its usual owner, and its evidence; later sections list the line items.

AreaCore requirementsCitationUsual ownerEvidence to keep
Privacy RuleNotice of privacy practices, minimum necessary, patient rights, privacy official, complaints, sanctions45 CFR 164.500 to 164.534Privacy officerNotice versions, request logs, complaint log
Security RuleRisk analysis and risk management, then administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for ePHI45 CFR 164.302 to 164.318Security officerRisk analysis, risk management plan, policies, access reviews
Breach Notification RuleBreach risk assessments and notices to individuals, HHS, and the media45 CFR 164.400 to 164.414Privacy officer with counselIncident log, risk assessments, copies of notices

Breach Notification Rule checklist

An impermissible use or disclosure of unsecured PHI is presumed to be a breach unless a documented risk assessment shows a low probability that the PHI has been compromised. The assessment weighs four factors: the PHI involved, who received it, whether it was acquired or viewed, and how far the risk was mitigated. Deadlines run from discovery: the first day the breach is known, or with reasonable diligence would have been known, to any workforce member or agent other than the person who committed it.

NoticeWho and whenCitation

MediaProminent media outlets serving a state or jurisdiction when the breach involves more than 500 of its residents, within the same 60 days164.406

State breach laws apply alongside HIPAA, so check the deadline in each state where your patients or members live before an incident.

Business associate agreement checklist

Sign a business associate agreement before a vendor creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI for you. Under 45 CFR 164.504(e) and the security terms in 164.314(a), every agreement must cover these terms:

  1. The permitted and required uses and disclosures of PHI, and no other use or disclosure except as the contract or the law requires.
  2. Appropriate safeguards, including compliance with the Security Rule for ePHI.
  3. Reporting of any use or disclosure the contract does not allow, including breaches of unsecured PHI under 164.410 and security incidents.
  4. The same restrictions for any subcontractor that handles the PHI.
  5. Support for individual rights: access under 164.524, amendment under 164.526, and the information needed for an accounting under 164.528.
  6. Compliance with the Privacy Rule for any covered entity obligation the vendor carries out.
  7. Availability of internal practices, books, and records to HHS.
  8. Return or destruction of all PHI at termination if feasible, and continued protection where it is not.
  9. The covered entity's right to terminate if the business associate violates a material term.

Keep a vendor inventory that shows which vendors touch PHI and when each agreement was signed and last reviewed. Your risk analysis still has to cover the risk each vendor brings.

Training, policies, and documentation

HIPAA requires training but sets no annual interval. Section 164.530(b) requires Privacy Rule training for each workforce member within a reasonable period after joining and after a material policy change, and 164.308(a)(5) requires a security awareness and training program for the whole workforce, including management. BD Emerson recommends training at hire and every year, because a dated annual record is easy to defend. Sections 164.316 and 164.530(i) and (j) require written policies and procedures, kept with the records they call for, for six years from creation or the date last in effect, whichever is later.

The risk analysis is the first required specification in the Security Rule and the subject of OCR's Risk Analysis Initiative, which had produced 14 enforcement actions by June 2026. It must assess the risks to all ePHI you hold and feed a risk management plan with owners and dates. Is there an official HIPAA certification?

No. HHS does not certify organizations as HIPAA compliant and does not recognize anyone else's certification. Its FAQ on Security Rule certification says "there is no standard or implementation specification that requires a covered entity to 'certify' compliance," that HHS "does not endorse or otherwise recognize private organizations' 'certifications'," and that a certification "does not preclude HHS from subsequently finding a security violation." When a vendor calls itself HIPAA certified, ask what stands behind the phrase. It usually means staff took a HIPAA course, an outside firm assessed the vendor against HIPAA, or the vendor holds a SOC 2 report or a HITRUST certification. Each is useful evidence, and none transfers the vendor's HIPAA obligations.

What are the penalties for HIPAA violations?

OCR sets civil money penalties in four culpability tiers under 45 CFR 160.404, adjusted each year for inflation. HHS published the 2025 adjustment in the Federal Register on January 28, 2026. The adjusted amounts apply to penalties assessed on or after that date for violations that occurred on or after November 2, 2015.

2Reasonable cause, not willful neglect$1,461 to $73,011$2,190,294
3Willful neglect, corrected within 30 days$14,602 to $73,011$2,190,294
4Willful neglect, not corrected within 30 days$73,011 to $2,190,294$2,190,294

Source: HHS, Annual Civil Monetary Penalties Inflation Adjustment, 91 FR 3665 (January 28, 2026).

Under a 2019 notice of enforcement discretion, OCR applies lower annual caps by tier: $25,000, $100,000, $250,000, and $1.5 million, each adjusted for inflation. Most enforcement ends in a resolution agreement with a payment and a corrective action plan. Of the actions dated January 2025 through September 2026 on HHS's resolution agreements page, two were civil money penalties, $1,500,000 against Warby Parker and $200,000 against Oregon Health & Science University.

Frequently asked questions

What is on a HIPAA compliance checklist? It covers the Privacy Rule, the Security Rule, the Breach Notification Rule, business associate agreements, workforce training, and written policies kept for six years. The Security Rule portion starts with a documented risk analysis and risk management plan, then the safeguards. Each line should name the requirement, its citation in 45 CFR Part 164, an owner, and the evidence that proves you meet it.

Who needs to comply with HIPAA? Covered entities and business associates. Covered entities are health plans, health care clearinghouses, and health care providers that transmit health information electronically in standard transactions such as claims. Business associates are vendors that create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI for a covered entity, including cloud providers that store encrypted data they cannot view, and their subcontractors.

Is HIPAA certification required? No. HHS does not require, issue, or recognize HIPAA certifications, and its FAQ states that a private certification does not prevent HHS from later finding a violation. Customers ask for third-party evidence instead, such as a HIPAA audit report, a SOC 2 Type 2 report mapped to HIPAA, or a HITRUST certification, which support your case without replacing your own risk analysis and records.

How often is HIPAA training required? HIPAA sets no fixed interval. The Privacy Rule requires training for each workforce member within a reasonable period after joining and after material policy changes, and the Security Rule requires a security awareness and training program. Training at hire and every year, with a dated record for each person, gives you evidence that holds up in an investigation.

What are the penalties for violating HIPAA? OCR's civil money penalties run in four culpability tiers. After the 2025 inflation adjustment published January 28, 2026, they range from $145 to $73,011 per violation in the lowest tier and from $73,011 to $2,190,294 in the highest, with a regulatory calendar-year cap of $2,190,294 for identical violations, which OCR's 2019 enforcement discretion lowers for the first three tiers. Most cases settle instead.

How long do you have to respond to a patient's request for records? A covered entity must act on a request for access no later than 30 days after receiving it under 45 CFR 164.524. It may extend once by up to 30 more days if it gives a written reason and a completion date within the first 30. Fees must be reasonable and cost-based, and OCR enforces the deadline, as its August 2026 Azul Vision settlement shows.
