What Is PCI Compliance? Merchant Levels, SAQ Types, and QSAs

Compliance
/
October 5, 2026

PCI compliance means meeting the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard (PCI DSS), the security standard for every business that stores, processes, or transmits payment card data, and proving it each year to your acquiring bank or the card brands. The PCI Security Standards Council (PCI SSC) writes the standard, now version 4.0.1, and the card brands and acquirers decide who must validate and how. Your merchant level, set by your annual transactions with each brand, decides the document: Level 1 merchants, above 6 million Visa transactions a year, need a Report on Compliance (ROC) from a Qualified Security Assessor (QSA), and most other merchants complete the Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ) that matches how they accept cards.

Who sets PCI DSS and who enforces it?

The PCI SSC writes and maintains PCI DSS, and the card brands enforce it. The Council published PCI DSS v4.0.1 on June 11, 2024 as a limited revision with no added or deleted requirements, and it retired v4.0 on December 31, 2024. Its FAQs state that the Council will not replace the brands' compliance programs and imposes no consequences for non-compliance. Each brand sets its own levels and runs its own enforcement.

For a merchant, the enforcer you deal with is the acquirer, the bank that contracts with you to accept card payments. PCI SSC tells merchants to ask their acquiring bank how to validate and tells service providers to ask the brands, and Visa's compliance page says acquirers must make their merchants validate at the right level and pay any assessment Visa levies. A merchant that stores, processes, or transmits card data for other businesses counts as a service provider too.

What are the PCI compliance levels?

PCI compliance levels rank merchants by annual transactions with each card brand, and the level decides the validation document. Visa and Mastercard share four thresholds, and a merchant that meets Visa's criteria for a level takes that level with Mastercard too, even when its Mastercard volume alone would place it lower. The table combines Visa's published merchant levels with Section 2.2.2 of Mastercard's Security Rules and Procedures (August 4, 2026 edition), as of October 2026.

LevelVisa transactions per yearMastercard and Maestro transactions per yearWhat you submit
Level 1More than 6 million, or a global merchant that any Visa region rates Level 1More than 6 million, or meets Visa's Level 1 criteria, or designated by MastercardAnnual ROC by a QSA (Mastercard also accepts an ISA), quarterly ASV scans, and an Attestation of Compliance (AOC)
Level 21 million to 6 millionMore than 1 million, up to 6 millionAnnual SAQ, quarterly ASV scans, and an AOC; Mastercard requires a QSA or ISA to validate SAQ A, A-EP, or D
Level 320,000 to 1 million e-commerceMore than 20,000, up to 1 million e-commerceAnnual SAQ and quarterly ASV scans; no validation to Mastercard required
Level 4Fewer than 20,000 e-commerce, and all others up to 1 millionAll other merchantsSet by the acquirer for Visa; for Mastercard, compliance with no validation to Mastercard unless a law requires it

A compromise moves merchants up the table. Visa can raise a compromised merchant's level, and Mastercard can designate a merchant with a confirmed compromise as Level 1, which changes the path from a self-assessment to a ROC, the largest single jump in PCI compliance cost. Mastercard's Site Data Protection page adds that acquirers report the status of their Level 1 and Level 2 merchants twice a year.

Where American Express and Discover differ

American Express and Discover count their own transactions. American Express sets Level 1 at 2.5 million or more American Express transactions a year and Level 2 at 50,000 to 2.5 million, both with annual validation and quarterly network scans, and merchants below 50,000 submit validation documents when American Express designates them. Discover uses three levels: 6 million or more Discover Network transactions, 1 million to 6 million, and all other merchants, which submit an SAQ-based AOC when Discover asks. A merchant with 3 million transactions on each brand is Level 2 for Visa and Level 1 for American Express, so check each brand you accept.

Service provider levels

Visa and Mastercard draw the service provider line at 300,000 transactions a year. Visa's Level 1 covers VisaNet processors and any service provider above 300,000 Visa transactions, which need an annual ROC by a QSA, and all others are Level 2 and complete an annual SAQ. Mastercard puts each third-party processor, payment gateway, token service provider, and 3-D Secure service provider at Level 1 regardless of volume, plus data storage entities and payment facilitators above 300,000 transactions. A service provider that self-assesses uses SAQ D for Service Providers, the only SAQ open to service providers.

PCI DSS v4.0.1 organizes its controls under 12 principal requirements, and each SAQ or ROC tests some or all of them. The titles below are the standard's own, from the PCI SSC document library.

What are the PCI DSS SAQ types?

An SAQ is the self-assessment form a merchant or service provider completes when its acquirer and the brands allow self-validation. PCI SSC publishes ten for v4.0.1, and each covers only the requirements that fit one way of accepting cards, so the narrowest SAQ you qualify for sets how much of the standard you evidence.

SAQWho is eligibleTypical example
SAQ ACard-not-present merchants that outsource all card data functions to PCI DSS compliant providers; the provider serves each element of the payment page by redirect or iframeCheckout redirects to a processor's hosted page
SAQ A-EPE-commerce merchants that outsource processing but whose own page serves the payment form or controls the redirectA direct post or JavaScript checkout form
SAQ BImprint machines or standalone dial-out terminals only, with no electronic storageA countertop terminal on a phone line
SAQ B-IPStandalone PCI-approved terminals connected to the processor over IP, with no electronic storageA countertop terminal on its own network link

SAQ P2PEOnly hardware terminals in a PCI-listed point-to-point encryption (P2PE) solution, with no electronic storageTerminals managed through a validated P2PE solution
SAQ SPoCA phone or tablet with a secure card reader that is part of a PCI-validated SPoC solutionA field service team taking payments on tablets
SAQ D for MerchantsAny merchant that fits no other SAQ, including any that stores card data electronicallyAn online retailer storing cards for repeat orders
SAQ D for Service ProvidersService providers that a card brand allows to self-assessA hosting provider below the Level 1 thresholds

SAQ A changed in this cycle. On January 30, 2025, PCI SSC published a revised SAQ A that removed Requirements 6.4.3 and 11.6.1 and the 12.3.1 risk analysis behind them, and added an eligibility criterion: the merchant confirms its site is not susceptible to attacks from scripts that could affect its e-commerce systems. The revision took effect on March 31, 2025. FAQ 1588 applies the criterion only to merchants that embed a provider's iframe, which meet it with their own script controls or with confirmation from their compliant provider, and redirect merchants fall outside it. FAQ 1604, published in June 2026, confirms that SAQ A merchants still owe quarterly scans by an Approved Scanning Vendor (ASV) under Requirement 11.3.2.

How to pick the right SAQ: BD Emerson's decision rule

Trace where card data travels in each payment channel before you open a questionnaire. BD Emerson works through five steps, in this order:

  1. Confirm your level with each brand you accept. If any brand rates you Level 1, or you are a Level 1 service provider, you need a ROC, and the SAQ question ends.
  2. List your channels, such as e-commerce, card present, and mail and telephone orders, and map each data flow from customer to processor, including any system that could change it.
  3. Look for stored card data. A database, spreadsheet, call recording, or backup that holds card numbers puts you on SAQ D for Merchants until that data is gone.
  4. Match each channel to the narrowest SAQ whose eligibility criteria it meets in full. A criterion met in part is a criterion failed.
  5. If channels land on different SAQs, ask your acquirer whether it wants one SAQ per channel or one SAQ D for all of them, and keep its answer in writing.

Step 3 carries the most weight, because one stored card number rules out all SAQs except D. Step 4 is where scope reduction pays: moving a checkout from a self-hosted form to a provider's hosted page moves that channel from SAQ A-EP or D to SAQ A.

ROC vs AOC vs SAQ: what each document does

The ROC and the SAQ are the two ways to document an assessment, and the AOC is the signed declaration of the result. PCI SSC describes the AOC as the document that attests to your compliance status.

DocumentWhat it isWho completes itWhere it goes
Report on Compliance (ROC)Full assessment report on PCI SSC's ROC Template, with a finding for each applicable requirementA QSA, or an ISA where the brand accepts oneKept by the entity and provided to the acquirer or brand on request
Self-Assessment Questionnaire (SAQ)Self-assessment of the requirements for one way of accepting cardsThe merchant or service provider, plus a QSA or ISA where a brand requires oneThe acquirer, with the AOC
Attestation of Compliance (AOC)Declaration of the ROC or SAQ resultSigned by an executive officer of the entity, plus the QSA or ISA when one performed the assessmentMerchants send it to the acquirer; service providers send it to the brands

What is a PCI QSA? QSA vs ISA vs ASV

A PCI QSA works full time for a PCI SSC validated QSA company and is authorized to perform PCI DSS assessments and prepare the reports brands and acquirers require. An Internal Security Assessor (ISA) works for a qualified ISA sponsor company and assesses its own employer, and an ASV runs the external scans in Requirement 11.3.2.

RoleWho it isWhat it doesWhen you need one
QSAFull-time employee of a validated QSA company, trained and tested by PCI SSCTests controls, samples systems, writes the ROC, and signs the AOCEach year for Level 1 merchants and service providers, and for Mastercard Level 2 merchants on SAQ A, A-EP, or D unless an ISA validates
ISAEmployee of a qualified ISA sponsor company, trained by PCI SSCAssesses its employer and validates its SAQs where a brand accepts thatWhen your brands accept ISA-led validation, as Mastercard does for Levels 1 and 2
ASVScanning company on PCI SSC's ASV listRuns external vulnerability scans and issues pass or fail reportsEach quarter, whenever Requirement 11.3.2 applies, SAQ A included

Check any QSA company against PCI SSC's QSA list before you sign. Penetration testers are a separate role: Requirements 11.4.2 and 11.4.3 call for a qualified internal resource or a qualified external third party with organizational independence, and the tester does not need to be a QSA or an ASV. How to tell which path your acquirer expects

Ask your acquirer in writing which level it assigns you and which documents it wants, because the acquirer sets your requirements and receives your AOC. Then check four conditions that change the answer.

First, another brand may rate you higher: Mastercard applies your Visa level when it is higher, and American Express starts Level 1 at 2.5 million of its own transactions. Second, you may qualify for a waiver. Visa's Technology Innovation Program lets eligible Level 1 and Level 2 merchants skip annual validation when at least 75 percent of transactions run through chip terminals or a PCI-validated P2PE solution, with no stored sensitive authentication data and no breach, and Visa's TIP application says those merchants must still comply with PCI DSS. Mastercard's Validation Exemption Program, in Section 2.2.4 of its rules, works in a similar way and excludes merchants that sell mainly through e-commerce without EMV tokens or through mail and telephone orders. Third, Mastercard's Level 2 rule brings a QSA or ISA into the SAQ. Fourth, a past compromise lets Visa and Mastercard raise your level. If the acquirer's answer differs from the brand tables, follow the acquirer, since its merchant agreement binds you, and ask which brand rule it applies.

How often do you have to validate PCI compliance?

You validate once a year with a new ROC or SAQ and a signed AOC, and several requirements run on shorter clocks. Visa requires service providers to demonstrate compliance at least every 12 months, and Discover sets the AOC due date one year after the prior validation.

Under the applicability notes for Requirement 11.3.2, an initial assessment does not need four passing quarterly scans if the latest scan passed, a documented process requires quarterly scanning, and rescans show the earlier findings fixed. From the second year, each quarter needs a passing scan.

Frequently asked questions

What is PCI compliance in simple terms? PCI compliance means your business meets PCI DSS, the security standard the card brands require of anyone who stores, processes, or transmits card data, and proves it each year. Merchants prove it to their acquiring bank with a Self-Assessment Questionnaire or a Report on Compliance plus a signed Attestation of Compliance, and most add quarterly scans by an Approved Scanning Vendor.

Who decides my PCI merchant level? Your acquirer assigns your level from each card brand's thresholds and your transactions over the past 12 months. Visa and Mastercard both start Level 1 above 6 million transactions a year, and Mastercard applies your Visa level when it is higher. American Express and Discover count their own transactions on their own scales.

What is the difference between PCI Level 1 and Level 2? Level 1 merchants, above 6 million Visa or Mastercard transactions a year, validate with an annual Report on Compliance from a Qualified Security Assessor, and Mastercard also accepts an Internal Security Assessor. Level 2 merchants, with 1 million to 6 million, complete an annual SAQ, and Mastercard requires a QSA or ISA to validate SAQ A, A-EP, or D.

Do small businesses have to be PCI compliant? Yes. PCI DSS applies to all merchants that accept cards, whatever their size. Most small merchants sit at Level 4, where Visa leaves validation to the acquirer and Mastercard requires compliance without validation to Mastercard unless a law requires it. Your acquirer can still require an annual SAQ and quarterly scans under your merchant agreement.

Which SAQ applies if I use a hosted payment page? A hosted payment page points to SAQ A when the provider serves each element of the payment page and no card data touches your systems. Redirect merchants fall outside the script criterion PCI SSC added in January 2025. Iframe merchants must confirm their site is protected from script attacks, and both still owe quarterly ASV scans.

What does a PCI QSA do? A Qualified Security Assessor works for a PCI SSC qualified QSA company and is authorized to assess merchants and service providers against PCI DSS. The QSA tests your controls, samples your systems, writes the Report on Compliance, and signs the Attestation of Compliance. Level 1 merchants and Level 1 service providers need one each year.
