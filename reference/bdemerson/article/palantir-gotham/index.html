Palantir Gotham: What It Is, Who Uses It, and How It Differs From Foundry

Technology
/
October 13, 2026

Palantir Gotham is Palantir Technologies' software platform for defense, intelligence, and law enforcement agencies. It pulls data from many sources into one model of people, places, organizations, events, and equipment, then gives analysts and operators the tools to search that model, map relationships, plan missions, and act on what they find, from an operations center out to the tactical edge. Palantir's product page calls Gotham "The Operating System for Defense Decision Making." It was the company's first platform, built after Palantir's 2003 founding for analysts at US defense and intelligence agencies. Commercial companies buy Foundry and AIP instead, which share Gotham's ontology-based design and its delivery infrastructure.

Facts in this guide are current as of October 2026. For the full map of Palantir's products and business model, see what Palantir does.

What does Palantir Gotham do?

Gotham turns records scattered across agencies and sensors into one picture an analyst can investigate, then supports the decisions that follow. Palantir's listing on the UK government's G-Cloud 14 marketplace describes Gotham as "an enterprise platform for planning missions and running investigations using disparate data," and its 2024 service definition document names the applications behind each capability. The table maps them.

CapabilityWhat it doesNamed tool
Data integrationIngests structured and unstructured data, including federated sources that update from the system of originBack-end and front-end integration tools
Object modelConverts records into objects with properties and links, which together form the OntologyOntology
SearchRuns one search across internal and external data sourcesBrowser, Object Explorer
Link analysisDraws networks of people, organizations, and events on a shared canvasGraph
Geospatial analysisPlaces objects on a collaborative map for command and controlGaia
Time analysisPlots the time ranges of objects, properties, and linksTimeline
CollaborationLets analysts co-write reports in real time and share findings with partner agenciesDossier, Chat, Inbox, Slides
Mission planning and targetingSupports targeting, fires control and execution, and intelligence, surveillance, and reconnaissance (ISR) workflowsAvailable from Palantir on request, not listed publicly
Security and auditControls access per object and per property, and keeps an audit trail that can be configured to be tamper-evidentPalantir security model
DeploymentRuns on public, private, and classified clouds, in multi-level security environments, and on edge hardwareApollo, Palantir's delivery platform

Two design choices separate Gotham from a search engine or a case management system. Every record becomes an object with typed properties and links, so an analyst works with one person or one vehicle instead of rows from a dozen databases. And Gotham enforces access on each object and each property, down to a single field such as a building's address, which is what lets agencies share one workspace across teams, partner organizations, and classification levels.

Palantir Gotham vs Foundry: how do they differ?

Gotham and Foundry apply the same core design to different buyers. Gotham serves government missions and investigations, and Foundry serves commercial and civil operations. Palantir's 2025 Form 10-K describes Gotham as used broadly across government functions and says all of Palantir's commercial customers use Foundry. Both platforms model data as an ontology, both are delivered by Apollo, and AIP runs on both.

How Gotham relates to the ontology, Foundry, and Apollo

The ontology is the idea Gotham introduced and Foundry inherited. A Palantir patent filed on November 20, 2006 and granted on June 14, 2011, US 7,962,495, "Creating data in a data store using a dynamic ontology", describes storing an ontology for a data store along with parser definitions that transform incoming data to fit the ontology's property types. That mechanism is how a signals report, a case file, and a vehicle registry land in Gotham as one set of typed objects. Palantir's ontology documentation uses the same vocabulary today and calls the ontology "the digital twin of an organization," built from object types, properties, link types, action types, and functions. Our Palantir ontology guide covers how those primitives work in practice.

The two platforms have also converged at the product level. Palantir's G-Cloud 14 service definition says Gotham "incorporates Palantir Foundry's capabilities," and the government authorizations covered below apply to Gotham and Foundry together.

Apollo is what makes Gotham deployable where defense customers need it. Palantir's 2025 Apollo solution overview describes automated delivery to "on-premise data centers, edge hardware and classified networks," including disconnected environments, and the 10-K describes Apollo as a single control layer for features, security updates, and configurations. For an agency, that means a Gotham instance on a classified network can take a security patch without a manual upgrade project. Our Palantir Apollo guide covers the hub-and-spoke model. AIP, Palantir's generative AI platform, runs in the same environments: Palantir's February 2026 announcement of PFCS Forward, described below, lists Apollo, Gotham, Foundry, and AIP as one authorized stack.

Where Gotham came from

Gotham grew out of counterterrorism work for the US intelligence community. Palantir's 2020 S-1 registration statement says the company was founded in 2003 and "started building software for the intelligence community in the United States to assist in counterterrorism investigations and operations," and that Gotham, its first platform, "was constructed for analysts at defense and intelligence agencies." The same filing says Gotham lets users "identify patterns hidden deep within datasets, ranging from signals intelligence sources to reports from confidential informants."

The early customer base followed from that origin. AFP reported that In-Q-Tel, the CIA's venture arm, began investing in Palantir in 2005. Michael Steinberger's 2025 history of the company, excerpted by Fast Company South Africa, identifies the CIA as Palantir's first major customer and dates the Los Angeles Police Department's relationship with Palantir from 2009 to 2021. The American Immigration Council reports that Palantir has supplied US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) with systems including FALCON and Investigative Case Management (ICM) since 2013. In 2019, Palantir took over the Pentagon's Project Maven work after Google withdrew, according to the same AFP report, and that work led to the Maven Smart System covered below.

How does the Maven Smart System relate to Palantir?

Maven Smart System, or MSS, is Palantir's software for the Pentagon's Maven program, which DefenseScoop reports began in 2017 to expand AI tools that detect, tag, and track objects in surveillance imagery from aircraft and satellites. Palantir took over the Maven work in 2019. In 2022, the Pentagon split Maven's management between the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency (NGA) and the Chief Digital and AI Office (CDAO), as DefenseScoop also reported.

The contract record is public. The Defense Department signed a $480 million, five-year indefinite delivery, indefinite quantity (IDIQ) contract for MSS in May 2024. In September 2024, Palantir announced a contract worth up to $99,804,561 over five years to extend MSS access to the Army, Air Force, Space Force, Navy, and Marine Corps. In May 2025, the Pentagon raised the MSS ceiling by $795 million to nearly $1.3 billion through 2029, and NGA's director confirmed more than 20,000 active Maven users. NATO's communications agency acquired MSS NATO on March 25, 2025. On March 9, 2026, Deputy Secretary of Defense Steve Feinberg directed that MSS become a formal program of record, with a CDAO program office taking over its administration from NGA, according to DefenseScoop. Our Maven Smart System guide covers what the system does, who uses it, and how the program and its contracts have grown since 2024.

Palantir describes MSS as "the connective tissue between operators and sensor feeds, platforms (hardware and software), and algorithms," supporting battlespace awareness, contested logistics, and joint fires and targeting workflows. Palantir's announcements present MSS as its own product and do not say which Palantir platform it is built on, so this article does not treat MSS as a version of Gotham.

The privacy and civil liberties criticism

The public criticism of Palantir centers on government uses of its software, and it falls into three areas: immigration enforcement, police data analysis, and the sovereignty questions raised when foreign governments rely on a US vendor.

Amnesty International concluded in September 2020 that Palantir was failing to conduct human rights due diligence around its ICE contracts, and named the ICM and FALCON systems. Germany's Federal Constitutional Court ruled on February 16, 2023 that the Hesse and Hamburg provisions allowing police to run automated data analysis violated the right to informational self-determination. The court named Hesse's hessenDATA platform, which Reuters reported uses Palantir software, declared the Hamburg provision void, and gave Hesse until September 30, 2023 to rewrite its law. The German civil rights group GFF has since filed a constitutional complaint against Bavaria's large-scale data analysis, according to The Local. In June 2026, AFP reported that governments and lawmakers in France, Germany, and Britain were rethinking their ties with Palantir, with foreign clients asking whether a US vendor would side with them or with the White House on sensitive decisions.

Palantir's position is that its software protects privacy and that it does not trade in data. Its 2025 Form 10-K states, "We are not in the business of collecting, mining, or selling data," and its G-Cloud listing names auditing of all user activity and built-in provisions for protecting data, privacy, and civil liberties among Gotham's features. Those controls govern who can see and change data inside the system. The criticism concerns which missions governments use the system for, which is a question of law and policy that each customer answers.

What should a commercial company take from Gotham?

For a commercial buyer, Gotham is evidence and a boundary. It is evidence that Palantir's ontology model and access controls have run on classified government networks. It is a boundary because every Palantir commercial customer uses Foundry, according to the 10-K, and AIP runs on Foundry. Frequently asked questions

What is Palantir Gotham used for? Defense, intelligence, police, and immigration agencies use Palantir Gotham to integrate data from many sources and act on it. Analysts run investigations, map networks of people and organizations, and analyze activity on maps and timelines. Military users plan missions and coordinate operations, from headquarters to the tactical edge. Palantir's 2025 Form 10-K describes Gotham as used broadly across government functions.

What is the difference between Palantir Gotham and Foundry? Gotham is Palantir's platform for government missions and investigations, and Foundry is its platform for commercial and civil operations. Both model data as an ontology of objects and links, both are delivered by Apollo, and AIP runs on both. Gotham's applications center on search, link analysis, geospatial command and control, and classified deployment. Foundry's center on data pipelines, operational applications, and write-back to business systems.

Can a private company buy Palantir Gotham? In practice, private companies buy Foundry and AIP. Palantir's 2025 Form 10-K says all of its commercial customers use Foundry, and Palantir positions Gotham for defense, intelligence, and law enforcement work. A company that wants Gotham-style capabilities, such as traversing links between entities or restricting access field by field, gets them through Foundry's ontology and security model.

Is the Maven Smart System built on Palantir Gotham? Palantir's public announcements about the Maven Smart System do not say which Palantir platform it runs on. Palantir markets MSS as its own product for the Pentagon's Maven program and describes it as connective tissue between operators, sensor feeds, platforms, and algorithms. Its contract ceiling reached nearly $1.3 billion through 2029 in May 2025, and a March 2026 directive set MSS on a path to become a program of record.

Is Palantir Gotham authorized for classified networks? Yes. Palantir Federal Cloud Service, which hosts Gotham with Foundry, AIP, and Apollo, received DoD Impact Level 6 provisional authorization for Secret workloads in October 2022 and FedRAMP High authorization in December 2024. In February 2026, DISA authorized PFCS Forward, which extends the IL5 and IL6 authorizations to on-premises and edge hardware.

Does Palantir Gotham collect or sell personal data? Palantir says it does not. Its 2025 Form 10-K states, "We are not in the business of collecting, mining, or selling data." Gotham integrates data that a customer agency already holds or is authorized to access, and the agency decides what goes in and who can see it. Critics, including Amnesty International and German civil rights lawyers, focus on how governments use the integrated data.
