Duck Creek Technologies: Products, Platform, and Guidewire Comparison

Technology
/
October 10, 2026

Duck Creek Technologies is a privately held software company that sells core systems to property and casualty (P&C) and general insurers: Duck Creek Policy for policy administration, Duck Creek Rating, Duck Creek Billing, and Duck Creek Claims, plus reinsurance, distribution management, payments, data, and agentic AI products around that core. Vista Equity Partners has owned Duck Creek since March 30, 2023, and Duck Creek delivers its products as software as a service (SaaS) through Duck Creek OnDemand, which it runs with Microsoft Azure. In April 2026, Duck Creek reported more than 370 customers and more than $150 billion of premium flowing through its platform each year.

We wrote this guide for carriers that have Duck Creek on a shortlist or run it today. We do not implement or resell Duck Creek, Guidewire, or any other core suite. Our insurance team includes people who helped lead a Guidewire implementation practice of thousands of professionals at a Big Four firm, and we help carriers compare suites with a custom agentic core on five-year numbers. Our guide to what Guidewire is covers the other half of that comparison.

Duck Creek Technologies at a glance

Duck Creek began in 2000 in Bolivar, Missouri, and today runs from Boston. The facts below change over time, so each carries a date.

Duck Creek history: Accenture, the IPO, and Vista

Duck Creek has changed ownership four times since 2011. Accenture announced on July 14, 2011, that it would acquire Duck Creek, which then served more than 60 clients in North America and the United Kingdom, and it completed the purchase on August 23, 2011, adding about 370 Duck Creek employees. Accenture then merged Duck Creek with one of its in-house software businesses, the Boston Globe reported. Duck Creek began selling SaaS core systems in 2013 and signed its first SaaS customer in 2014, according to its fiscal 2020 Form 10-K.

On April 18, 2016, Accenture and Apax Partners announced a joint venture in which Apax-advised funds would take 60 percent of Duck Creek and Accenture would keep 40 percent, and Duck Creek dates its return to independence to August 2016. Duck Creek priced its initial public offering of 15 million shares at $27.00 on August 13, 2020, and its stock began trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the symbol DCT the next day.

Vista Equity Partners agreed on January 9, 2023, to buy Duck Creek for $19.00 a share in cash, a deal valued at about $2.6 billion and a 46 percent premium to the January 6 closing price, and it completed the take-private on March 30, 2023. Mike Jackowski, chief executive since 2011, became vice chair in October 2025, when Hardeep Gulati, formerly chief executive of PowerSchool, took over. Duck Creek then launched its Agentic AI Platform on April 28, 2026, and on July 3, 2026, it acquired Send, a London underwriting software company founded in 2017.

How Duck Creek OnDemand and Active Delivery work

Duck Creek runs its products for customers as SaaS. Its press releases still use OnDemand as the name for that service, while its Cloud Delivery page now names three delivery models, one of them OnDemand. Its security page says OnDemand runs with Microsoft's Azure cloud services, maintains an ISO 27001-certified information security management system, and undergoes annual SOC 1 Type II and SOC 2 Type II examinations.

ModelHow Duck Creek describes itWho it is for, per Duck Creek

Active Delivery changes how upgrades work. When Duck Creek made Policy with Active Delivery generally available on August 5, 2024, it promised bi-weekly product updates applied automatically, each behind a feature flag that the insurer turns on when ready. The Reinsurance with Active Delivery release of April 8, 2026, describes monthly updates applied in the middle of each month. That release also says the reinsurance product is built on AWS, while Duck Creek's reinsurance product page says its products deploy on Microsoft's Azure cloud. We treat the difference as a contract question: confirm the cloud and region for every module and every AI service before you sign.

How Duck Creek configuration works

Duck Creek keeps configuration separate from platform code. Its fiscal 2022 Form 10-K says "the configuration layer is separated from our solutions and platform code," and it says technical and non-technical users tailor the products with low-code tools. Duck Creek Policy calls its approach Product Factory: business and IT teams model coverages, rules, and rating logic together, configure a product once, and reuse or inherit that definition when they launch the next one. Our policy administration system guide explains what a policy administration system does, compares the main vendors, and gives a scoring checklist for choosing one.

The configuration artifacts are called manuscripts. Duck Creek's Agentic Product Configurator, launched April 29, 2026, reads underwriting manuals, rating guides, and forms, then produces "implementation-ready manuscript configurations" for Duck Creek Policy, with human review at decision points. Duck Creek reports early results of up to a 50 percent cut in requirement and manuscript effort, and its product page lists the tool in Controlled Availability through Duck Creek Professional Services.

Partner integrations show the toolchain a carrier's developers need. Duck Creek's Content Exchange listing for its Snapsheet claims integration requires Microsoft Visual Studio and SQL to install. Duck Creek University certifies client and partner staff at Proficient and Master levels. In our view, the number of extensions a carrier carries outside the standard model decides a Duck Creek program, because heavy extension moves it from Active Delivery toward Active Delivery Select.

Duck Creek implementation: partners, timelines, and pricing

Systems integrators and Duck Creek's Professional Services run implementations. Its fiscal 2022 10-K counted 23 systems integrators with more than 6,000 Duck Creek implementation resources. Duck Creek's partner awards for 2024 and 2025 went to integrators including Accenture, Cognizant, Xceedance, LTIMindtree, Hexaware, Next Level Solutions, and Aggne, a Wipro company, and Coforge delivered Argyle's launch.

Public timelines come from Duck Creek and its customers, and they describe the fast end. The Cloud Delivery page says implementations of 3 to 6 months are possible, and Argyle Insurance rolled out its first product through Australian brokers on Policy, Billing, and Rating in under 60 days. Duck Creek's last 10-K gave no typical duration, while Guidewire's fiscal 2026 Form 10-K says implementation and testing of its products "typically lasts six to 24 months or longer." Duck Creek does not publish prices. Its last 10-K as a public company, for fiscal 2022, said it generally priced SaaS and license arrangements at individually negotiated rates based on the direct written premium (DWP) its solutions manage, "with pre-determined fee adjustments as the customer's DWP increases over the term of the contract." If a contract applied one flat rate to DWP, 8 percent annual premium growth would raise the year-five fee by 47 percent, because 1.08 to the fifth power is 1.47. Tiered rates soften that curve, so the tier table matters more than the year-one price, as our five-year business case for replacing Guidewire shows.

When Duck Creek fits, and when to look elsewhere

Duck Creek fits carriers that want a full P&C suite run as SaaS, accept a standardized operating model in exchange for continuous updates, and work comfortably in a Microsoft environment. It also fits carriers that want one module, such as Reinsurance or Clarity, and multi-country insurers in the markets Duck Creek supports today, which include North America, the United Kingdom and Ireland, India, Australia, and New Zealand, according to its June 2026 platform announcement.

Look at another suite when your requirements fall outside that footprint, for example when you want life and annuity administration from the same vendor, which Sapiens and Majesco both sell. Look at a custom agentic core when your products are where you compete, premium growth keeps raising a DWP-based fee, and you want AI agents to act on policy, billing, and claims data under permissions and logs your team controls. Recent Andersen Consulting insurance work includes platforms that enabled the launch of two new insurance companies in less than a year each, and our policy administration system practice scopes that option against the suites.

What to ask in a Duck Creek evaluation

These seven questions surface the cost and risk that demos skip.

  1. Which delivery model, Active Delivery, Active Delivery Select, or OnDemand, will each module run on, and what does moving between models cost?
  2. Which cloud and region host each module you are buying, including Reinsurance and each agentic application, and which model provider processes your data?
  3. How long can a feature flag stay off, and what regression testing does Duck Creek run against your configuration before each update?
  4. What are the DWP tiers and fee adjustments for each year of the term, at today's premium and at plan premium?
  5. Which of your products and states does Industry Content cover today, and who loads each bureau circular?
  6. Which integrator will staff the program, how many Master-certified people will it name in the contract, and who maintains your manuscripts after go-live?
  7. Which agentic applications are generally available, and can your own agents read and write core data through the AI Gateway?
